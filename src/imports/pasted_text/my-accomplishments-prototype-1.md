Create a mobile app prototype section called **My Accomplishments**. This is a PRIVATE space for a teen to notice, record, and look back on their own accomplishments, strengths, and things they are proud of. Nothing in this section is shared with other users. Do not include likes, comments, leaderboards, social feeds, or public profiles.

The goal of this section is to help users broaden what they value about themselves and remember positive things they have done, including very small everyday accomplishments.

Keep the wording below as closely as possible. Do not make the language overly clinical or motivational. The design should feel simple, warm, teen-friendly, and private.

## SCREEN 1 — MY ACCOMPLISHMENTS HOME

Title:
**My Accomplishments**

Short description:
**Take a minute to notice something positive about yourself or something you've done.**

Show TWO large activity cards that the user can choose from.

### Activity Card 1

Title:
**Word of the Day**

Description:
**Guess today's positive word, then think of a time you've shown it.**

Include a small Wordle-style visual preview with letter tiles.

Button:
**Play**

### Activity Card 2

Title:
**My Accomplishments**

Description:
**Keep track of things you've done that you're proud of — big or small.**

Show a preview of a private board with several personal accomplishment cards or sticky notes.

Button:
**View my board**

Also include a small privacy indicator near the top:
🔒 **Only you can see this**

## ACTIVITY 1 — WORD OF THE DAY

Create a simple Wordle-style game using a positive trait or strength word.

Example word:
**BRAVE**

Allow the user to guess the word using letter tiles.

After the user solves the word, show:

**Today's word is BRAVE**

Then ask:

**Think of something you've done recently that showed bravery.**

Add helper text:
**It can be something big or small.**

Include a text box:
**What did you do?**

Placeholder:
**Write a few words or a sentence...**

Include an optional button:
**I need an idea**

If selected, expand a small section with examples such as:

* Spoke up when I normally wouldn't
* Tried something new
* Asked someone for help
* Did something even though I was nervous
* Stuck with something that was difficult

Then allow the user to save their response.

Button:
**Add to My Accomplishments**

After saving, show a short confirmation:

**Added to your accomplishments ✓**

Give the user two choices:

**Pin this**
**Done**

If they pin it, add it to the Pinned section of their private accomplishments board.

---

## ACTIVITY 2 — MY ACCOMPLISHMENTS BOARD

Create a private personal board where the user can keep track of things they are proud of.

At the top include:

🔒 **This board is just for you.**

## ADD AN ACCOMPLISHMENT

When the user selects “Add an accomplishment,” show:

**What's something you've done recently that you're proud of?**

Helper text:
**It doesn't have to be a big achievement. Small things count too.**

Text box:
**What did you do?**

Placeholder:
**Write a few words or a sentence...**

Include:
**I need an idea**

When selected, show:

**Think about something you did...**

* At school
* With friends
* With family
* In a sport, hobby, or activity
* For yourself
* That was difficult
* That helped someone else
* That you kept trying at

Then return to the response box.

Button:
**Add to my board**

After saving, ask:

**Want to keep this one at the top?**

Buttons:
**📌 Pin it**
**Not now**

## INTERACTION AND DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

Make all screens clickable as a prototype.

The user should be able to:

1. Choose either activity from the My Accomplishments home page.
2. Complete the Word of the Day activity.
3. Enter an example of how they showed that strength.
4. Save it to their accomplishments board.
5. Pin or unpin accomplishments.
6. Add an accomplishment directly without completing Word of the Day.
7. Open the “I need an idea” prompts when they have trouble thinking of an answer.
8. Navigate back to the My Accomplishments home screen.

Keep the experience short. Most screens should have only one main question or action.

Use rounded cards, clear buttons, large readable text, and a playful but not childish design appropriate for teens ages 13–17.

Most importantly, make the accomplishments board feel like a **private personal collection**, not a social media feed.
